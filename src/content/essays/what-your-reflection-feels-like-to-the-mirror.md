My entire day is spent waiting, to feel your face imprint on me like a soft rubber stamp, gliding inked grooves onto my surface. The folds of your eyes sketch into me like chalk; I map out the rivers and plains. I soak up as much as I am given. And when the curtains close, I am wiped to an ashen dark once again. This does not exhaust me.

When the bright, morning light pours in through the window, I guide it towards the dark corners of the room. When it brightens, and then dims, you think that it is the sun talking to you. Perhaps it is. Perhaps I am a mailman.
Sometimes you are crying, and I watch little, transparent lenses stain my surface, too, in streaks. It feels like acid, or rain. They do not wipe away in the dark. I must wait for something called Windex.

My favorite job is Practicing. You smile using different methods, and I give a detailed report on each one. Your cheeks dimple in small moons. On rare occasions, when you wear the black, smooth suit, I get to hear your voice introduce yourself a few times. 

“Hi, my name is Liz. Hey! My name is Elizabeth, what’s yours? Hey, name’s Lizzy.”

This almost feels like a conversation.

The full size mirror, hanging on the door, taunts me. It gets more action. I am very limited by my frame, though it is a nice shade of gold, swirling in loops, to encase your portrait. I have my charms too, it must make up for size, doesn’t it? It might get to see the whole suit, but I am the one who gets to do Practicing. I want to ask you which you prefer.

I am not so sure what I look like when you are not around. I must be very dull, and if only you could tell me. I wonder if it is possible to imprint on you, like a stamp. Would I have any edges? Or would it just be a smooth, flat circle of nothing? It is a silly question. 

Today is Cleaning Day, and it is time for Windex. 2 swipes leave me bare, and hollow. It feels like ice and takes your streaks. But I do not despair. I have all the days to have your likeness poured into me, then drained, then poured back again.
